## The two things every other guide gets wrong

**They quote legroom.** Legroom is the easy number to find and the easy problem
to solve — seat rails are long, and almost every car here clears 41 inches.
Headroom is the one fixed by the roof, and it is what actually stops tall
drivers getting comfortable. If you are raising the seat to see over the dash
and running out of ceiling, no amount of legroom saves you.

**They ignore the sunroof.** Only two manufacturers in this entire guide
publish both figures, and both of them show a penalty over an inch:

- **Subaru Outback** — 40.1" without the moonroof, **39.1"** with. A full inch.
- **Volkswagen Atlas** — 41.3" without, **40.1"** with. An inch and a quarter.

Everyone else quotes one number, and it is always the flattering one. There is
no reason to think Honda, Ford or BMW have solved a problem Subaru and
Volkswagen could not — they simply do not print the second figure. Assume
roughly an inch wherever glass is fitted.

At 6'4" that inch is the entire decision, and it means **the trim you test
drive may not be the trim you buy.** It also means a car can pass on paper and
fail in the showroom, which is how most tall drivers end up thinking the spec
sheets are useless.

There is a third factor with no published spec at all: **steering wheel
telescoping range**. A car with generous legroom and a short column still
forces you to sit closer than your legs want. It can only be found by sitting
in the car, which is why some entries below carry a question instead of a
number.

## Price does not buy headroom

This is the counterintuitive part, and the data is unambiguous. A **$30,000
Bronco Sport gives a tall driver four and a half more inches of headroom than a
$75,000 Audi Q8.** A used Honda Fit beats a new Mercedes E-Class by more than
two inches.

The reason is shape, not budget. Luxury SUVs and sedans chase a sloping,
coupe-like roofline and fit a panoramic roof as standard — two decisions that
both come directly out of your head space. Boxy, upright, unfashionable cars
win, and they are usually the cheap ones.

## Check the year, not just the model

The BMW 3 Series is the clearest warning here. The previous generation had
**40.3 inches** of front headroom. The current one has **38.7** — a inch and a
half gone in a single redesign, with the badge and the model name unchanged.

Every guide still quoting 40.3" is recommending a car you can no longer buy.
A model that fit your father does not necessarily fit you, and "the 3 Series is
good for tall drivers" stopped being true without anyone updating the advice.
Check the figure for the exact generation you are shopping.

## How to shop with this

1. **Filter on headroom first,** then check legroom. Reverse that order and you
   will test drive a lot of cars you do not fit in.
2. **Test drive the exact trim you intend to buy.** A sunroof is a different car
   for you, and dealers rarely think to mention it.
3. **Check the model year,** not just the model. Headroom moves between
   generations and the name stays the same.
4. **Check the steering column travel** while you are sitting in it. It is the
   one thing no spec sheet will tell you.
5. **Ignore price as a proxy for space.** The data says it is close to
   backwards.
