## Seat height is a proxy, not an answer

Every listing quotes a seat height, every buyer compares them, and the number
is only half the story. Two bikes with an identical 800mm seat can feel
completely different at a stop, for three reasons nobody prints:

**Seat width.** The seat spreads your legs before they ever reach the ground.
A wide adventure-bike saddle can cost you more effective reach than 20mm of
height. This is why a tall, narrow dirt bike is often easier to flat-foot than
a lower, broad-seated tourer.

**Suspension sag.** A soft bike drops 20–40mm the moment you sit on it. The
published figure is unladen, so a plush cruiser measures taller than it rides
and a stiff sportbike measures closer to the truth.

**Where the pegs are.** For tall riders the ground is rarely the problem —
knee angle is. A low seat with high, rearset pegs folds your legs up, and that
is what makes a bike unbearable at hour three. Seat height alone will never
tell you this.

So treat the tables below as a shortlist, not a verdict. The only real test is
sitting on the bike.

## Rough guide by inseam

Match your **inseam**, not your height — two riders at 5'10" can differ by
three inches in the leg. Measure from crotch to floor in the boots you ride in.

- **Under 29"** — you want roughly 760mm or less to get both feet down
- **29–32"** — 760–815mm puts you in the widest part of the market
- **32" and over** — 815mm and up, and you should be filtering on legroom
  rather than reach

These are bands, not cutoffs. Plenty of riders happily run a bike one band
above with a boot sole, a lowering link, or simply one foot down — which is
all most experienced riders use anyway.

## If nothing fits

Seat height is one of the few fit problems you can actually fix:

1. **Lowering links** drop most bikes 25–40mm for under $150.
2. **A shaved or low seat** costs less than you would expect and does not
   affect handling.
3. **Boots with a thicker sole** buy an easy 15–25mm.
4. **Suspension sag adjustment** can drop a bike without changing any parts,
   though it trades away some travel.

None of these help with the tall rider's problem, which is knee angle. For
that you are looking at peg lowering kits or a taller seat — the opposite
direction to everything above.

## Help fill this in

This covers the bikes measured so far. A lot of common models still have no
seat height recorded, and the ones missing are mostly the bikes people
actually shop for. If you own one and know the figure, that is the fastest way
to make this guide more useful.
